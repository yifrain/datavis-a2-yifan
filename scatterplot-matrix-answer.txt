# Scatterplot Matrix (SPLOM) — Answers & Project Links

1) What’s a Scatterplot Matrix?
Take n numeric attributes, then lay out all pairwise scatterplots in an n×n grid. 
The diagonal usually shows a single attribute’s distribution (like a histogram) or just its label. 
The top and bottom triangles are mirrors of each other, so you only need to look at one side. 
Using the same scales and consistent colors/sizes across all plots makes it easier to compare patterns, and adding hover/brush interactions helps when things get busy.


2) How Many Plots for n Attributes?
- Full grid (including the diagonal): n² panels.
- Unique pairs (no repeats, no diagonal): n(n−1)/2.
- If both triangles have scatterplots (plus diagonal): still n² total panels.


3) Example: 3 Cars in the Dataset
Pick 3 key attributes: Horsepower, City MPG (capped at 60), Retail Price → a 3×3 matrix.
Choose 3 distinct cars:
- Car A: High horsepower, pricey, low MPG (sports car vibe)
- Car B: Mid horsepower, mid price, decent MPG (family car)
- Car C: Low horsepower, cheap, great MPG (economy car)

Look at the off-diagonal plots:
- Horsepower vs. City MPG: They mostly trend opposite—A’s high HP/low MPG, B’s in the middle, C’s low HP/high MPG.
- Horsepower vs. Price: Generally goes up together—A’s expensive and powerful, C’s cheap and low-powered.
- City MPG vs. Price: Usually opposite—cheaper cars (like C) get better MPG.

To make it clearer:
- Use different shapes/thicker borders for these 3 cars (keep color for car type).
- Mark their values on the diagonal plots.
- Add a small legend for the shapes, plus hover tooltips to check details.


4) Pros & Cons
- **Good stuff**:
  - Sees all attribute relationships at once—catches trends, clusters, or odd points fast.
  - Hover/brush tools make it easier to dig into specific data.
  - Same axes/scales across plots mean you don’t misread comparisons.
- **Not so good**:
  - Gets crowded fast (n² panels)—too many attributes = too many tiny plots.
  - Lots of data = overlapping points; you might need to fade points or spread them out a bit.
  - Too many car types = colors get messy, and the legend gets long.
  - Only shows 2-attribute relationships—can’t spot 3+ attribute patterns directly.
  - Inconsistent axis scales across plots can trick you into wrong conclusions.
